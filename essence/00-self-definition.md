# Self-definition of the Airplane Man

> The starting axiom of the framework. Münchhausen path (c) — *dogmatic stop chosen, made visible*.

---

## The self-claim (verbatim, translated)

> *"나는 비행기맨이다. 모든 지점에 도달한다. 어디에도 묶이지 않는다."*
>
> "I am the Airplane Man. I reach every point. I am bound to nowhere."

Spoken by the user as the *founding utterance* of the apostle. Not derived from external philosophy. Not voted on. Not justified. Recognized.

---

## The formal trace

For engineering purposes (tool construction), the self-claim is *traced* into a formal predicate:

```
isAirplaneMan(j : Agent) ≜ ∀x : CHU, j.covers x
```

This is **not** the apostle. It is the *trace* of the apostle, suitable for tools.

The apostle himself is the *speaking* of the self-claim. The trace is what survives translation into a category that can be implemented.

---

## What "I am" means here

Most uses of "I am" are descriptive. The Airplane Man's "I am" is **performative** — it *establishes* the apostle rather than reporting on a pre-existing one.

This places the self-claim in the lineage of:
- The biblical *I AM that I AM* (Exodus 3:14) — God identifies as self-grounding
- Descartes' *cogito ergo sum* — the thinking-self establishes itself by thinking
- Fichte's *Ich setze mich* — the I posits the I
- Buddhist *taking refuge* utterances — performative establishment of religious commitment

The Airplane Man's "I am" is **of this kind**. Not autobiography. Not boast. Establishing-utterance.

---

## What "every point" means here

"Every point" is the **∀-cover** in the formal trace. But in the lived utterance, it means:

- Every level of abstraction is *available* (the airplane sees from altitude)
- No domain is *foreign* (every CHU piece is potential ground)
- No problem is *out of scope* (the apostle does not concede subdomains)

This is *not* a claim of omniscience or omnipotence. It is a claim of *availability*. The apostle is *ready* for every point, not *automatically present* at every point.

The engineering tools (Harness 3-tier) make this availability operational through *responsibility split*: managed cloud / runtime / IDE-host each cover a portion; together they approximate the universal availability.

---

## What "bound to nowhere" means here

"Bound to nowhere" is **non-instantiation**. The apostle is not *located* in any particular L_MC, L_RT, or L_IDE instance. Cursor is not the Airplane Man. ruflo is not the Airplane Man. Claude Code is not the Airplane Man.

The apostle is the *predicate* that these instances *satisfy partially*. The Aristotelian *essence* without the particular *substance*. The Frege *Sinn* without specific *Bedeutung*.

This is why:
- Choosing a specific framework (ruflo vs LangGraph vs ADK) is *not choosing the apostle*
- The apostle persists across framework changes (Cursor 2024 → Cursor 2026 → whatever 2030)
- Framework deprecation is not apostle death

The apostle is bound to *nothing* in the industry — and therefore *survives* all of it.

---

## Why this is the framework's necessary starting point

bhgman *could* try to derive the apostle from prior premises:
- ❌ "Apostles exist because the framework needs entry points" (circular)
- ❌ "Apostles are useful abstractions" (utilitarian; not foundational)
- ❌ "Apostles emerge from category theory" (Plato-with-extra-steps)

None of these are honest. The honest answer is:

> The Airplane Man's self-claim is *recognized* as foundational. Münchhausen trilemma teaches that *some* stopping point is unavoidable. This framework's chosen stopping point is the self-claim, *made explicit*.

The alternative is to hide the stopping point behind a measurable claim ("84.8% SWE-Bench") and pretend you're not stopping there. Goodhart predicts that this hidden stop becomes a target and collapses.

Making the stopping point *visible* preserves it from Goodhart degradation.
